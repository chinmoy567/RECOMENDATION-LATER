# Writing a strong academic reference letter (Canadian grad admission)

Distilled from guidance by UVic, McGill GPS, Waterloo, Alberta, Calgary, Western, York, Guelph.

## Principles
- Specific beats superlative: replace "hard-working, brilliant" with an example and outcome.
- Strengths early; don't bury awards or publications.
- Comparative evidence: "top 3 of 120 in my 12 years", percentile vs peers.
- Describe field norms (typical master's timelines, publication expectations) so readers can calibrate.
- Name the exact program; date it; 1-2 pages (about 500-800 words); letterhead, signature, institutional email.
- Honest: one growth point framed constructively. Uniform praise is distrusted.
- No irrelevant personal data (age, religion, marital status).
- Consistent Canadian spelling; plain, confident English.

## Structure
1. **Opening**: who I am, how long and in what capacity I know the candidate, how many students I have taught, strength of recommendation.
2. **Academic standing**: courses, grades, rank, Bangladesh grading context (see lor-bangladesh-context).
3. **Two evidence paragraphs**: one research/project story (role, independence, result), one on character/teamwork/leadership. STAR style.
4. **Comparison and potential**: vs peers and past students admitted to strong programs; why ready for graduate work in this field.
5. **Honest growth point** (optional, brief).
6. **Close**: clear recommendation, fit with program, offer to be contacted.

## Upgrades
- Weak: "He is hardworking." Strong: "He rebuilt the model three times after reviewer feedback and presented it at a national conference."
- Weak: "Good student." Strong: "Ranked 2nd of 110; the only student to solve the extension problem."

## Output
The draft letter plus a list of [VERIFY] items the referee must confirm.
