# Recommender brief (applicant -> referee)

Strong letters are specific, and referees can only be specific if reminded of concrete evidence. This skill produces a brief, not the letter. The referee writes and owns the letter.

## Choose referees
- Prefer professors who taught/supervised you closely (thesis/project supervisor > course teacher > department head). A famous name who barely knows you gives a weak letter.
- Mix types per program rules (see lor-program-research). Use an industry supervisor only if the program accepts professional referees.
- Ask: "Can you write a *strong* letter for me?" and give an easy way to decline.

## Brief contents (2-3 pages)
1. Polite request email: program names, deadlines (ask 4-8 weeks ahead), portal instructions, thanks.
2. Snapshot: target programs, why each, research area, career goal.
3. Relationship: courses with grades, dates, thesis/project title, how long they know you.
4. Evidence bank (3-5 items) in STAR form (Situation, Task, Action, Result) with a number (rank, marks, accuracy, users, hours). Include one difficulty and how you grew.
5. Strengths to corroborate, mapped to committee criteria: research ability, independence, analysis, communication, teamwork, resilience.
6. Context the referee is best placed to give: rank/percentile, batch size, grading strictness, any CGPA dip and why.
7. Attachments: CV, transcript, statement of purpose draft, publication/project links.
8. Gentle reminder one week before the deadline.

## Rules
- Never write the final letter for the referee to sign. Offer facts, not finished prose.
- Be truthful; every claim must be checkable by the referee.
