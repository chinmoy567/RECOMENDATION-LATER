# Humanizer: natural, referee-voiced English

Goal: make the letter sound like one specific person who really knows the candidate. This is about quality and voice, not about hiding who wrote it. The referee must still verify and adopt every sentence.

## Remove AI tells
- Words: delve, tapestry, testament, pivotal, crucial, vibrant, landscape, realm, foster, leverage, showcase, underscore, multifaceted, meticulous, seamless, unwavering, passion for, commendable, "stands out as".
- Patterns: "not only X but also Y", rule-of-three lists in every paragraph, "In conclusion/Overall", em-dash overuse, a closing sentence that restates the paragraph, "It is worth noting that".
- Uniform sentence length and perfectly balanced paragraphs.
- Stacked adjectives and praise with no event behind it.

## Add human texture
- Mix short and long sentences. Allow one slightly informal, personal line ("I still remember the day she...").
- One concrete, slightly unglamorous detail per example (the late-night lab, the failed first run, the exact course code).
- First person, direct opinions: "I rank her among the best five students I have taught."
- Plain verbs over fancy ones. Contractions are fine in moderation for some referees; match the referee.
- Imperfect symmetry: paragraphs of different lengths.

## Match the referee
If a writing sample from the referee exists (email, past letter), mirror their usual vocabulary, formality, and sentence length. Keep Canadian/British spelling consistent.

## Do not
- Add invented anecdotes or facts to sound human. Only reuse verified evidence.
- Strip out the specific numbers and ranks.
- Promise that the text will pass any AI detector. Detectors are unreliable; the real test is whether the referee would say it this way.

## Output
Revised letter, a short change log (tells removed, lines made more personal), and [VERIFY] flags kept intact.
